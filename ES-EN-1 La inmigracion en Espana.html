Marking principles applied

MARKING PRINCIPLES — translation into English
Misspelling is tolerated as long as it does not lead to ambiguity.
Variants on the answers listed are acceptable if they convey the same intended meaning.
The translation is successful if an English speaker would understand it without having read the original.
Where two alternatives are offered and both are correct the mark is awarded; if one of them is incorrect the mark is withheld.
Repeated errors, including the definite article, are penalised once only.
How this page marks. It checks that every element of an acceptable answer is present — in any order, allowing synonyms, tolerated accent errors and minor stem misspellings — and withholds the mark when the Reject column applies or a required element is missing. The real mark scheme also credits any appropriate alternative a human examiner judges correct, and no program can reproduce that. So where you disagree, read the mark scheme for that chunk and award yourself the mark with a justification. Your download shows both totals and your reasoning, and your teacher decides.
